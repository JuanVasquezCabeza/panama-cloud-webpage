Every year your institution decides how much to invest in admissions and how to split it across social media, search, radio, billboards, fairs and open days. At the end of the cycle the enrollments arrive, but it is rarely clear **how much of that result each channel brought in**.

That question has an answer, and it doesn’t depend on following every family around the internet. It’s called **Marketing Mix Modeling (MMM)**.

## What Marketing Mix Modeling is, in plain words

It is a statistical model that starts from what you already have: how much you invested in each channel each week or month, and how many leads, applications and enrollments you got over the same period. Using that aggregate data, the model separates what each channel contributed from what would have happened anyway. It also accounts for the fact that a campaign’s effect is not immediate, that every channel saturates, and that admissions have seasons.

You don’t need to install trackers, identify families, or depend on what each platform reports about itself.

## Why it matters for an educational institution

An enrollment is worth years of tuition. A school whose students stay six years, or a university with multi-semester programs, can justify far more analysis per enrollment than a one-time purchase business. Moving the admissions budget well, even by a small percentage, pays for itself.

Admissions also have traits that generic marketing models ignore: a single decision window per year, channels that work for months before a family asks, a lot of traditional media (radio, billboards, fairs), and leadership that wants to know whether the intake target is at risk.

## Where to start

You don’t need perfect data. Assessing what data exists and how useful it is is exactly the first step: the **marketing budget and data audit**. It ends with a clear recommendation and a plan to implement the model, so you decide with evidence.
