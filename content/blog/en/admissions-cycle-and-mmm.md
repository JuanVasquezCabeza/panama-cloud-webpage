Admissions don’t work like a store. A family doesn’t decide on an impulse: they take months to think, visit, compare and wait for the enrollment window. Because of that, **when** you invest matters as much as **how much**, and it is the main reason simple reports mislead.

## Effects arrive late

A radio campaign in September doesn’t produce applications in September. It builds awareness that turns into visits in November, applications in January and enrollments in March. Reports that compare each month’s spend with that month’s results will tell you the radio did nothing, and the following months’ numbers will credit someone else.

Marketing Mix Modeling represents this explicitly as a **carry-over effect**: an investment keeps working for weeks or months, fading gradually. Each channel has its own duration; an open day lasts a different time than a billboard.

## A single window per year

Most institutions have one big decision window. A large share of enrollments is concentrated in a few weeks, and the rest of the year builds toward it. A model that ignores seasonality would credit whatever channel happened to be running during the peak, when the peak would have come anyway.

> Illustrative example, not data from any client. A school invests heavily in digital ads in the weeks before enrollment opens, and applications spike. A simple report says digital is the best channel. The model separates the seasonal peak from the effect of the ads, and finds that part of the spike would have occurred regardless, while the radio spend months earlier explains a share that the report attributed to digital.

## What this means in practice

- Comparing years requires accounting for the calendar: an early open day or a change in the enrollment date moves results between months.
- The best time to adjust the mix is months before the window, not during it.
- Each admissions cycle adds information; with two or more cycles the model separates seasonality from channel effects much better. If you have fewer, the initial audit evaluates what is feasible.

## Where this connects

Lagged effects are why the answer to “which channels bring students?” (first guide) can’t come from the last click: the real cause happened months earlier. And seasonality gives the enrollment forecast (fourth guide) its shape, with its peaks and valleys.
