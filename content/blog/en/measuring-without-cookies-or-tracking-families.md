For years, digital marketing measurement relied on following people: a cookie here, a tracking pixel there, a click that could be tied to an application form. That foundation has been eroding, and for an educational institution the problem is bigger than for most.

## What stopped working

- **Browsers and phones limit tracking.** Safari and Firefox restrict third-party cookies, and iPhone apps must ask permission to track, which most people decline.
- **Consent and regulation.** Across Latin America, data protection rules are tightening (for example, LGPD in Brazil), and an admissions team that handles data about families and minors has good reasons to be careful.
- **Platforms grade their own homework.** Each platform reports its own results with its own attribution rules, and the totals rarely add up to the real number of enrollments.
- **Offline is invisible.** Radio, billboards, fairs and open days leave no click to follow, yet they often drive the decision.

## What MMM uses instead

Marketing Mix Modeling needs only **aggregate data**: how much was invested per channel per week, and how many leads, applications and enrollments there were that week. There are no individuals in that data, no cookies, no identifiers. It doesn’t matter whether a family accepted tracking, used a private browser or heard about the school from a neighbor.

That brings two benefits at once: the measurement still works as tracking keeps shrinking, and the institution handles far less personal data to get an answer.

> This is a design property of the method, not legal advice. Your institution’s obligations depend on the country and on the rest of how it handles data; the audit only needs aggregated figures.

## What it doesn’t replace

MMM doesn’t tell you what a specific family did, and it doesn’t optimize individual ads in real time. It answers a different question, the one the budget depends on: **which channels bring students, and where to put the money.** Click-level tools remain useful for day-to-day campaign management; MMM is what you use to check them.

## Where this connects

This is why the first guide’s question, which channels bring students, can no longer be answered with click attribution alone: the clicks are missing or biased. MMM measures contribution without them.
